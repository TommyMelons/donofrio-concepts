# D'Onofrio Homepage Research

Research completed August 20, 2026. The current site confirms that D'Onofrio is a Brooklyn-based heavy civil, infrastructure, marine, roofing, and building restoration contractor with family roots in concrete masonry. That real operating profile guides these concepts. Residential renovation language is not used as a substitute for the company's actual work.

## Sites reviewed

- [D'Onofrio General Contractors](https://www.donofrio.biz/index.html): Established the real company story, disciplines, family roots, and current content gaps. The site relies on long text blocks and gives projects, equipment, and field work too little visual weight.
- [D'Onofrio Marine](https://www.donofrio.biz/marine.html): Verified the West Midtown Ferry Terminal project and supplied approved-site project photography for these concepts.
- [D'Onofrio Substation](https://www.donofrio.biz/substation.html): Verified the Mott Haven, Astor, and Newtown substation projects and supplied their existing project photography.
- [D'Onofrio Roofing](https://www.donofrio.biz/roofing.html): Verified the Con Edison 74th Street Turbine Roof Replacement project and its existing photography.
- [Turner Construction](https://www.turnerconstruction.com/): Uses a short human headline, a full-screen project image, clear audience paths, and repeated proof through projects, commitments, and local presence.
- [Gilbane](https://gilbaneco.com/): Pairs family ownership with global capability. The useful lesson is that heritage works best when it supports current operations.
- [Structure Tone, STO Building Group](https://stobuildinggroup.com/structuretone): Organizes credibility around locations, leaders, sectors, and measurable operating facts.
- [Skanska USA, Heavy Civil](https://www.usa.skanska.com/what-we-deliver/build/infrastructure/heavy-civil/): Uses disciplined sector navigation, plain service labels, and infrastructure imagery that communicates scale quickly.
- [Sciame Construction](https://sciame.com/): Lets highly designed project photography carry the page, with restrained typography and minimal interface chrome.
- [Hunter Roberts](https://hrcg.com/): Leads with a direct claim, then backs it with a compact statistics row and an image-led project sequence.
- [Plaza Construction](https://www.plazaconstruction.com/): Uses motion and large project panels, then balances them with concise operating principles and market categories.
- [Scordio Construction](https://scordio.com/): Shows how a Brooklyn family business can present craft and longevity with a premium visual tone.
- [Snøhetta](https://www.snohetta.com/): Treats the homepage as a changing visual index. Large imagery, simple captions, and generous space make the work feel important.
- [BIG](https://big.dk/): Uses the portfolio itself as navigation. Dense project imagery works because labels stay short and filtering is immediate.
- [SHoP Architects](https://www.shoparc.com/): Uses a compact point of view and confident typography instead of long promotional copy.
- [Awwwards, Soletanche Bachy Experience](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/soletanche-bachy-experience): Site of the Day. The recognized work uses scroll-led project navigation, strong transitions, and a restrained red, gray, and white palette.
- [Awwwards, OH Architecture homepage](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/homepage-oh-architecture): Recognized for an editorial grid, typographic hierarchy, horizontal work browsing, and a draggable gallery.
- [SiteInspire, Building and Construction](https://www.siteinspire.com/websites?categories=220): The strongest examples use big type, minimal grids, and project-first homepages rather than generic service-page templates.
- [SiteInspire, Maben](https://www.siteinspire.com/website/13389-maben): A useful big-type, minimal construction reference with a simple visual system.
- [Recent, formerly Godly](https://godly.website/): Reinforces the current move toward bold type, modular image feeds, and motion that helps browsing rather than decorating the page.
- [Unsplash, heavy-lift crane barge](https://unsplash.com/photos/a-large-crane-on-a-barge-X-kS3DTrKUA): Reviewed as a marine reference, then removed from Concept 3. The photograph shows a foreign harbor, so it is not safe to put in front of the D'Onofrio family.

## Anti-references

- [Current D'Onofrio site](https://www.donofrio.biz/index.html): We are not repeating the small-image slideshow, long undifferentiated paragraphs, weak project hierarchy, or desktop-era spacing.
- [SAS Roofing and Waterproofing](https://www.sasroofingwaterproofing.com/): We are not repeating duplicated service blocks, generic sales copy, crowded sections, or a homepage that makes every service compete at the same visual level.

## What carries across

1. Put real work first. One strong field image and one clear sentence outperform a collage and a paragraph.
2. Make proof scannable. Projects, disciplines, equipment, credentials, and verified numbers should appear as labeled facts close to the top.
3. Use motion with a job. Sticky navigation, project filtering, and restrained reveals can improve orientation. Decorative animation should not slow down estimating or qualification.

## Photography note

Project sections now use photography and project names published on D'Onofrio's current marine, substation, and roofing pages. Remaining hero, crew, and field images are free stock placeholders. Replace those with approved D'Onofrio yard, fleet, crew, family, and active-site photography before launch. No AI-generated people are used.
